# HR_Attrition_Analytics-LookerStudio_Project
# 1. Project Overview

The HR Attrition Analytics project analyses employee information to understand workforce composition and employee attrition. The dashboard presents key HR metrics, including total employees, employees who left, average monthly income and average employee age. It also explores attrition across departments, gender, job roles, business travel, job satisfaction, income brackets and age groups.

The report contains two main dashboard views:

HR Attrition Analytics Dashboard – an overview of employee headcount, attrition split, department-level leavers, gender distribution, job roles and business travel.

Advanced Employee Attrition Analysis – a deeper comparison of attrition rates by department and gender, job satisfaction, income bracket and age group.

The aim is to help HR teams identify workforce patterns and areas that may need further investigation. The dashboard describes associations in the available employee data; it does not establish why an individual employee leaves.

# 2. Business requirements
• Display headline workforce KPIs.

• Show the proportion of employees who left and stayed.

• Compare employee exits by department and job role.

• Analyse attrition rates by demographic and employment-related groups.

• Provide filters for overtime, department, job role, age group and income bracket.

• Present results in a format that HR stakeholders can use for review.

# 3. Tools and technologies
• Primary tool: Google Looker Studio, Google Sheets and Microsoft Excel.

• DataSet: HR data.

• Dashboard Tools: KPI scorecards, bar charts, pie charts, tables, calculated metrics, and interactive filters.

# 4. Challenges faced
• Comparing attrition fairly requires checking both the exit count and the employee population in each group.

• Small groups, particularly some department-and-gender combinations, can produce volatile percentages.

• Chart labels provide approximate readings in some views; avoid presenting those estimates as exact metrics.

• Attrition patterns show association, not proof that age, income or satisfaction alone caused an employee to leave.

# 5. Key findings
• There are 1,470 employees and 237 exits; the overall attrition rate is 16.12%.

• Sales has the highest department attrition rate (20.63%), followed by Human Resources (19.05%). Research & Development is lower at 13.84%.

• Overall attrition is 17.01% for male employees and 14.80% for female employees.

• Within HR, female attrition is 30.00% and male attrition is 13.95%; interpret this carefully because the group sizes may differ.

• The dashboard charts suggest higher attrition among younger employees and employees in the lower income bracket. These are visual estimates, not exact table        values.

• The job-satisfaction chart suggests attrition is higher in the Low satisfaction group and lower in the Very High group.

# 6. Recommendations
• Review workload, incentives, career progression and manager support in Sales, which has the highest department attrition rate.

• Investigate the HR gender difference with employee counts and confidential qualitative feedback before taking action.

• Prioritise retention conversations and career-development support for younger and lower-income employee groups.

• Monitor job satisfaction through regular pulse surveys and follow up on low scores.

• Track monthly attrition rate, regrettable exits, tenure bands and retention-action outcomes.
